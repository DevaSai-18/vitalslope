See how fast a patient is changing, not just where they are.

VitalSlope follows heart rate, temperature and SpO₂ in real time. Differential calculus turns each reading into a direction and a speed, so a trend shows up before a limit is crossed.

A reading tells you where. A derivative tells you where it’s heading.

The rate of change between two readings is a slope. Make the gap between them smaller and that slope settles on the true instantaneous rate, dP/dt. Drag the sliders and watch it happen.

Live monitor

A simulated patient produces a reading every second, standing for one minute. Each vital relaxes toward a target by dP/dt = k(target − P). We estimate its rate of change from the last six readings and warn when a limit is crossed or about to be.

Where it fits, and where it stops

Differential calculus gives a useful way to study how readings change over time. This site is a teaching prototype.

Applications

  • Teaching patient-monitoring concepts
  • Remote-monitoring prototypes
  • Wearable data visualisation
  • Time-series research and dashboards

Limitations

  • Simulated data only
  • Simplified model; sensor errors not modelled
  • Not for diagnosis or treatment

Future scope

  • Validated sensor data under supervision
  • Better filtering and rate estimation
  • Secure data handling and expert review
VitalSlope is an educational microproject using simulated data. It does not diagnose or make medical decisions.
